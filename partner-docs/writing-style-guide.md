## Voice-refinement patterns (transferable techniques, learned from live use)
These are *methods* for keeping outreach in your voice, not scripts. Fill the brackets with your own phrasing.
- **Protect your signature idiom.** If you have a go-to opening line or phrase that sounds like you, keep it in full; don't let the assistant compress or "improve" it away. When it gets flattened, restore the original.
- **Warmth over precision in the mirror.** When complimenting the reader, a warm, human phrasing often beats an exact statistic. Offer the precise figure, but it's fine to loosen it for tone.
- **Match the hook to the reader.** For a mission-driven / nonprofit boss, it's often better to drop a clever thesis-hook entirely and open with genuine warmth about their impact, then your leadership philosophy. Save the sharper "here's the problem I obsess over" hook for product/infra readers.
- **Generalize an over-specific hook.** If an opening line gets too company-specific, trim it back to your core, punchier version rather than a bespoke one-off.
- **Use a direct, role-targeted connector** when there's a live role (e.g. "I'm interested in your [role]") instead of a vague "I'm reaching out about."
- **Keep your sign-off consistent** — the same name + personal link/site every time, so it reads as unmistakably you.

> Honesty note: voice ≠ facts. Facts come from `PROFILE.md`; never reuse a claim you can't defend.


## Zero AI tells (HARD RULE — all job-search materials: résumé, cover letter, outreach)
Write in your voice with ZERO AI tells. Audit every résumé, cover letter, and email against this before presenting it as ready.
- **Banned filler adverbs:** actually, honestly, genuinely, truly, really, simply, just (as filler), very, quite, arguably, notably, importantly, ultimately.
- **Banned AI-cliché verbs/adjectives:** leverage (verb), delve, utilize, seamless(ly), robust, cutting-edge, vibrant, meticulous, showcase, underscore, testament, boasts, tapestry, myriad, plethora, foster, empower, elevate.
- **Banned AI-cliché phrases:** "I was impressed", "passionate about", "proven track record", "results-driven", "detail-oriented", "in today's fast-paced world", "it's worth noting", "at the end of the day", furthermore, moreover.
- **Punctuation tells:** no em dashes; no spaces around slashes.
Principle: cut the qualifier and state the thing plainly. When in doubt, delete the adverb. Prefer your lexicon (builder, ship, 0-to-1, outcomes, friction, data distribution, hands-on).

## Résumé summary ≤ 300 characters (hard cap)
The Summary/profile line on the 1-page résumé must be ≤ 300 visible characters. Count it programmatically after building; if over, trim to the strongest role-relevant proof.
