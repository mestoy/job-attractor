# Your writing samples, the voice corpus

> **This file is a TEMPLATE. It is empty on purpose and it is the most valuable thing you will fill
> in.** `scripts/voice_samples.py` reads it, `scripts/check_outreach.py` points at it, and the
> outreach co-construction flow calls its Stage 2 mandatory: three phrasings **in your voice**.
> With this file empty, that stage produces three phrasings in a generic register, which is the
> one failure the whole voice apparatus exists to prevent.

## ⏱ Fill this in before your first outreach, not after

It takes about 15 minutes and it is a copy and paste job. You are not writing anything new. Open
your sent mail and your LinkedIn messages and take what is already there.

## What to collect, and why these five

Paste **5 to 8 real messages you sent.** Aim for one of each shape, because the pipeline
picks samples by TYPE and a corpus of five cold emails cannot teach it how you sound in a reply:

| Shape | Where to find it | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| A cold note to a stranger | sent mail, any outreach | your opener and how you introduce yourself |
| A favor asked of a friend | messages to someone you know well | your warm register, which is a different person |
| A reply to someone who answered you | any thread with a back and forth | how you carry a conversation rather than start one |
| A thank-you | after an interview, a favor, an intro | your gratitude, which is easy to fake and easy to spot |
| Something you wrote in public | a LinkedIn post or comment, your website, a bio | ⚠️ your PUBLIC voice is not your message voice, and mixing them is a common miss |

⛔ **Do not clean them up.** Typos, running sentences, a joke that half lands: that is the data.
A polished version teaches the pipeline how you WISH you sounded.

## How to add one

Copy the block below, paste the message verbatim, and say one line about the situation. The
situation matters as much as the words, because a sentence carries the relationship it was written
into.

```
## Sample N - <what it was> (<date>, <who it went to, and how well you know them>)

Setup:
  <one or two lines: what prompted it, how close you are to this person, what you wanted>

Verbatim (copy the VOICE, never the facts):
  │ <paste the message exactly as sent, including the greeting and the sign-off>

Cadence lessons:
  <optional at first. Fill this in later, after you notice a pattern across several samples.>
```

## After a few weeks

The corpus gets far more useful when it records **your edits**. Whenever you rewrite a draft before
sending, save both versions and note what you changed. The gap between the draft and
the version you sent is the single richest signal about your voice, and it is invisible from the sent
message alone.
