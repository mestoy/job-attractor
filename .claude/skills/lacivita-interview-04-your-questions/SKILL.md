# Stage 4 — the questions you ask

> Index and ground rules: `lacivita-interview-pipeline.md`. Andy's original wording is not
> reproduced here; build the candidate's own in their voice.

## What this stage is for

Interviewers weigh your questions as a direct signal of preparation, judgment, and genuine
interest, and many decide on them. This stage builds a researched question sequence the candidate
owns, ordered so the conversation widens naturally: the company's direction first, the role's
reality second, the boss's world third. It is also half of how the CANDIDATE evaluates the
employer; a question you already know the public answer to is wasted in both directions.

## The sequence

**Company first.** Questions that prove you studied them and are thinking about their business,
not your seat. The strongest form starts from something specific you observed (a launch, a
statement, a public number) and asks the question the observation raises. An "I noticed X, and it
made me wonder Y" question cannot be asked by someone who did no work, which is exactly why it
lands.

**Role second.** Questions that surface the seat's reality: what the first six months must
produce, what makes the difference between good and great in it, where it has been hard. These
answers are also your screening data; listen for the gap between the JD and the answer.

**Boss third.** Questions about how the boss works and what they need: how they like to work with
this seat, what would make them look back in a year and call the hire a win. Asked plainly, these
read as maturity, and the answers tell you whether you want this boss.

## Mechanics

- Three to five questions prepared, expecting time for two or three. Order them so the best two
  come first.
- Every question carries its research: the observation it starts from, with the source. A
  question without a source is a generic question, and generic questions score as no questions.
- Never ask about compensation, benefits, or logistics in an interview round; those belong to the
  process conversation with the recruiter, after interest is established.
- Write down what you actually want to LEARN from each question, so a partial answer can be
  followed up instead of nodded at.

## Building the sequence for a specific interview

1. Research pass: the company's recent moves (launches, funding, public statements), the seat's
  JD read closely, the interviewer's public work. Verify each fact at its source before it
  anchors a question; a question built on a wrong fact is worse than none.
2. Co build the questions with the candidate in their words, each anchored to one verified
   observation. Offer directions, let them choose and phrase.
3. Sanity pass: cut any question whose answer is on the company's website, and any question that
   is really a statement wearing a question mark.
4. The final three to five, ordered Company, Role, Boss, go on the stage 5 day of card with their
   sources.

Output: three to five researched questions in the candidate's words, ordered, each with its
source noted.
