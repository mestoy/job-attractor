# LaCivita Boss-Hunt Email — Line-by-Line Checklist (run EVERY time)

> **MANDATORY pre-fire gate.** Before opening the mail client / building any boss-hunt draft, run the finished draft through EVERY line below and **report ✅/⚠️/❌ (or partial) per item to the candidate** — visible, so a miss can't hide. Fix ❌/⚠️ or surface them for a decision before building the draft. This operationalizes Andy LaCivita's boss-hunting method + the candidate's writing voice.

## Part A — LaCivita technique (the method)
- **A1. Right recipient.** Addressed to the WOULD-BE BOSS / hiring manager (the product lead or would-be peer-lead over the role), whose CURRENT role is verified — NOT HR, NOT a recruiter, NOT the CEO (CEO only if the org is flat AND the founder owns product).
- **A2. Goal 1 — why + specific praise.** A clear reason for writing PLUS genuine, RESEARCHED praise of the boss or company: a real, verifiable, *specific* accomplishment (ideally in their own framing). Not generic ("I love your mission"), and not merely a shared-interest statement — it must point at something THEY did.
- **A3. Two-sided praise.** Their specific accomplishment is mirrored by a SPECIFIC accomplishment of the candidate's (their thing ↔ your comparable thing), so the praise is earned, not flattery.
- **A4. Goal 2 — who you are + value to THEM.** The candidate's identity and the concrete value they bring, tied to the boss's problem/role, with at least one hard, defensible proof (one strong proof beats three vague ones).
- **A5. Goal 3 — enthusiasm + ONE ask.** Genuine enthusiasm and a single, low-friction ask (a short conversation / on your radar). One ask only; no desperation.
- **A6. Value-forward + confident.** Framed around what the candidate brings, not what they need. No begging, no over-explaining, no apology.
- **A7. Short + scannable.** ~3 beats / 4–6 lines; reads in one screen.
- **A8. Authentic + human.** Sounds like a curious person, not a résumé or a template.
- **A9. One touch, then move on.** **EMAIL ONLY on first contact** (résumé attached). ⛔ **NEVER a LinkedIn connection request before contact** — the bare-connect pairing is RETIRED and is a hard-invariant violation. ⚖️ **A cold boss who does not answer gets NO second touch** — follow-ups are for people the candidate has a **warm relationship** with; for a stranger the next action is a NEW target. LaCivita, Boss Hunting Bible p.9: *"Generally, I'm not much for following up"*; p.10: *"You will benefit much more from reaching out to new people than chasing individuals who are either not getting back to you."* When a WARM follow-up is warranted: **forward the original email + two lines**, 7-10 days out, "say as little as possible" (p.9). LinkedIn only if the contact is **verified 1st-degree**, since a 2nd-degree contact cannot be messaged at all.
- **A10. Résumé attached**, tailored to the role.

## Part B — The candidate's voice (writing-style guide)
- **B1. Greeting** with the name and a warm tone (e.g. "Hi, [First]!" / "Hey, [First]!") per the candidate's style.
- **B2. NO em dashes** anywhere (commas, ellipses …, parentheticals instead).
- **B3. NO spaces around any forward slash** (`0-to-1`, `React/TypeScript` — never ` / `).
- **B4. NO banned / never-suggest words** — filler adverbs and AI clichés per the candidate's writing-style guide (common never-suggest set: "exactly", "actually"). Audit option PREVIEWS too, not just the final text.
- **B5. 3-beat structure** — hook (researched detail about THEM) → credential (the candidate + a concrete proof) → ask (warm, light).
- **B6. Concrete over vague** — defensible numbers; one strong proof over three weak.
- **B7. Tools named** — reference the candidate's real tools/differentiators by name where relevant.
- **B8. Signature** — the candidate's name + their site link.
- **B9. No retired/undefensible claims** — every figure/claim traces to a verified primary source (honesty guardrails).
- **B10. Body fits ~1 screen** — no wall of text.

## How to report
Present a two-column ✅/⚠️/❌ table (Part A rows, Part B rows) per email. Any ⚠️/❌ gets a fix or an explicit "keep as-is?" decision BEFORE the draft is built. **Most common real finding: A2/A3 fail when the hook is a shared-passion statement rather than praise of a specific thing the boss actually did** — catch it here.
