# Stage 1 — the one minute introduction

> Index and ground rules: `lacivita-interview-pipeline.md`. Andy's original wording is not
> reproduced here; build the candidate's own in their voice.

## What this stage is for

"Tell me about yourself" opens most interviews, and the first minute sets how everything after it
is heard. The goal is not a résumé recital. It is a short, forward pointing story that tells the
interviewer three things: who you are professionally, what you are great at, and why that matters
to THEM, ending somewhere that invites the next question you want.

## The shape

Build it in this order. Sixty to ninety seconds spoken, no more.

1. **Present:** one sentence on who you are professionally right now, in plain words.
2. **A thread, not a tour:** the two or three career beats that explain how you got here, chosen
   because they point at this role, not because they are chronological. Skip everything else.
3. **The strength:** the one thing you are demonstrably great at, said as a claim you can defend
   with a story from stage 2.
4. **The proof breadcrumb:** one concrete, verifiable detail that makes the strength real (a
   shipped thing, a number, a customer outcome). One. More than one turns it into a list.
5. **The bridge to them:** why this strength meets what this company needs right now. This is the
   sentence most people omit, and it is the one that does the work.
6. **The invitation:** end pointing at the thing you most want to be asked about next.

## Scenarios this opener must survive

- **"Walk me through your résumé" instead.** Same opener, slower thread. Do not switch to a
  chronological recital; the shape holds.
- **The interviewer who already read everything.** Compress steps 1 and 2 to one sentence each and
  spend the minute on strength, proof, and bridge.
- **A screen call with a recruiter, not the boss.** Keep the shape, drop the depth. The recruiter
  is listening for the seat's keywords and for coherence, not for the proof chain.
- **A founder interview.** The bridge sentence carries more weight: founders hear hundreds of
  self descriptions and remember the one that was about their company.

## Building the candidate's opener

Co build it beat by beat, in the candidate's own words, through explicit choices:

1. Read the candidate's profile and their most recent writing samples first, then draft NOTHING.
2. For each of the six steps, offer the candidate a choice of directions grounded in their real
   record, and let them supply or pick the wording. One step at a time.
3. Time the assembled opener read aloud. Over ninety seconds, cut a beat from step 2, never the
   bridge.
4. Have them say it aloud until it survives being interrupted after any sentence. An opener you
   can only deliver start to finish is not learned yet.

Output: the opener, written down, in their words, plus the step 6 invitation noted so stage 3 can
arm the story it points at.
