# Skill: line-by-line outreach construction (human-in-the-loop)

> **Governs every message type**, not just cold-boss email bodies. Cold boss-hunt emails use the
> full 6-beat build below. **Follow-ups, replies, and warm intros** use the **two-stage-per-message**
> flow in the next section. In all cases: **build the message WITH the user, never hand them a
> finished draft** (single or batch). Every line is a decision they make, not one you make for them.

## Follow-up / reply / warm intro — two-stage-per-message

A follow-up is essentially one beat (the bump), so it maps onto the praise two-stage as a whole:

1. **Stage 1 — angle (3 options, EACH with a one-line sample).** Draw the angles from the SPECIFIC
   prior note's hook (pull it from the outreach log) so each is one-of-a-kind, never a generic
   "bumping my note." E.g. plain-bump vs. value-reminder (re-surface the exact role/thesis) vs.
   light-and-brief. The user picks the angle.
2. **Stage 2 — phrasing (MANDATORY, 3 options in the user's voice).** From their writing samples and
   sent corpus, offer 3 phrasings of the chosen angle. They pick the wording. **Never author a
   finished message right after the angle pick and present it as done** — that is the skipped-Stage-2
   miss, and it has happened: a user approved only the CONCEPT, not the text, and a finished draft
   was shown as if already approved.
3. **Assemble, then the user sends.** LinkedIn message (the second touch; email was the first), or
   an email bump where they are not connected. NEVER a cold connection request. Then log the second
   touch and clear any follow-up-due marker.

Use a structured picker (three named options) for each stage, one message at a time. Replies to a
boss's inbound message use this SAME two-stage flow; any seed scenarios in a response playbook are
starting concepts, never canned sends.

⚠️ **A follow-up has no scorecard**, so it should not need the same build-gate ruling a cold draft
needs — but a preview checker that cannot tell a follow-up from a cold draft would block it anyway.
If this kit's gate needs a non-forgeable exemption, put `FOLLOWUP: <Company>` in the picker's
question text and let the gate confirm that company is already recorded as sent in the outreach
log (so a cold draft mislabeled as a follow-up to a never-contacted company still blocks). The
banned-word/format lint on the previews still runs regardless.

## Cold boss-hunt email (the 6-beat build)

**When:** a match is approved and the user says to prep or build the outreach. This is how the
email body gets written, one line at a time, the user choosing every line. It replaces "draft the
whole email and let them edit" with "build it with them, line by line."

**Why:** maximum control, and it teaches their voice fastest, since every selection is high-signal.
It also forces LaCivita's method to be honored line by line, not just overall.

## Order of operations

1. **Pick the approach first** (already done before this skill runs): role-targeted vs.
   founder-radar; email-first with résumé attached; channel + any BCC-hedge plan.
2. **Then build the email LINE (or beat) AT A TIME.** For each beat below, present exactly 3 options
   in the user's voice (per their writing-style guide and their sent samples), each honoring that
   beat's LaCivita function. **The user selects one (or edits via "Other").** Lock it, then move to
   the next beat. Assemble the final email from their selections.
3. Use a structured picker for each beat's 3 options (or a review console). Recommend one option
   (first, "Recommended"). Keep the running email visible as it assembles so they see it in context.
4. **Never fire the send until every beat is selected.** Then: primary `To` + any BCC hedges,
   résumé attached, the user sends.

## The beats (LaCivita element → 3 options in the user's voice)

1. **Greeting** — their normal opener (e.g. "Hi, [First]!"). 3 light variants at most; usually just
   confirm.
2. **Hook / why-this-company** (LaCivita: a genuine reason you're writing). Draw from their real
   motivation for this specific company, not a generic template line.
3. **Boss-specific praise** (LaCivita's #1 element) — this is the two-stage pick: **Stage 1**, the
   accomplishment angle (3 interest-aligned options, EACH shown with a one-line sample of the
   drafted beat so the pick is never blind); **Stage 2, MANDATORY**, 3 phrasings in the user's voice
   of the chosen angle. The praise TEXT is approved ONLY by the Stage-2 pick, never
   assistant-authored-then-shown-as-final. Genuinely specific about what THE BOSS did (researched
   per the boss-research skill), mirrored by a specific accomplishment of the user's. Log the
   Stage-2 pick to a decision log; require the approved phrasing to appear verbatim in the body
   before any send.
4. **The user's offer / credential** (LaCivita Goal 2) — their identity + ONE matched concrete proof
   from their real work history, honest figures only. Optionally a leadership-philosophy line for
   mission-driven targets.
5. **The ask** (LaCivita Goal 3: enthusiasm + a direct ask) — role-targeted: firm "I'd love to talk
   about [specific]." Radar (no live role): "I don't see a role that fits me open right now, so I'd
   love to be on your radar for when one does."
6. **Sign-off** — name + personal site link, not just a bare first name.

## Honesty + voice guardrails (every beat)

- Facts only from the user's verified profile document. No invented metrics, no rounding up, no
  claim that outruns what is actually documented.
- No em dashes; no spaces around slashes. Warm, short, specific to the reader.
- Boss-praise credits what the boss actually did (don't imply they founded something they only
  lead, or led something they only joined).

## Shorthand

If the user says "just draft it" or is moving fast, offer the full 3-options-per-beat build OR a
single assembled draft, their call. Default to line-by-line when they're constructing deliberately;
offer the one-shot draft when they want speed.

## Capture their picks (durable learning loop)

Record each beat pick (plus any edits and the inferred preference) to a decision log as you build.
When the same preference shows up across several outreaches, promote it to a standing rule in their
writing-style guide, citing the decision log. This is how the options get more in-voice over time.
Present all beat choices as an interactive picker, one beat at a time.
