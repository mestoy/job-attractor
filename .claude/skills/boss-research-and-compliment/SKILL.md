# Boss Research & Compliment skill

## Steps

0. **Precondition: only run this after the user has confirmed the match from their scorecard.**
   Screening happens first from data (no browser tabs open); this skill's browser-opening steps run
   *only* once they say "pursue."
0a. **Research the accomplishment to a rigorous standard FIRST**, before proposing any praise beat.
    A secondhand claim is a false-positive risk: disambiguate attribution (THIS boss, THIS company,
    THIS role, and get the verb right), verify the accomplishment or metric on a **primary source**
    (company blog, the boss's own talk, a named case study, a filing, never a data-broker blurb or
    an SEO blog; company-sourced product accomplishments are valid when the boss has no personal
    public presence, since earnings calls, press releases, and product launches attributable to the
    org they lead count). If a metric is secondhand-only and the primary source is silent on it,
    **drop the metric** and use the qualitative accomplishment the primary source supports. Carry a
    verbatim citation string with the source URL. No primary-source citation means the beat is not
    ready.
0b. **Always read the user's writing-style guide (and skim recent sent-message samples) BEFORE
    writing any compliment or message option.** Every option must carry their actual voice markers,
    not generic praise: their normal greeting shape, their real hooks and phrasing habits, short
    witty human sentences. Avoid AI tells (below) and consultant-speak like "what gets me" or "I was
    impressed."
1. **Open the sources in the user's browser** (boss LinkedIn and activity, company site, any notable
   essays or podcasts) so they see the evidence themselves.
2. **Find at least 3 "intersection points"** — things that are BOTH (a) verifiable, publicly
   self-disclosed accomplishments or viewpoints of the boss, AND (b) genuinely interesting to the
   user because they map to who the user is. Public info only; never infer identity; frame every
   similarity positively.
3. **Propose at least 3 compliment APPROACHES** (distinct angles), each a drafted line, noting the
   LaCivita fit (does it praise a *specific accomplishment*? that's the #1 element) and why the user
   would like it. **Praise the accomplishment in the user's own words; do NOT parrot the boss's own
   phrases back** — a light paraphrase at most, since quoting the boss too much reads as flattery.
   - **ONE praise beat, genuinely specific on BOTH sides**: pair the boss's *specific* accomplishment
     with a *specific* accomplishment of the user's that mirrors it, pulled from their real work
     history, honest figures only. Specific beats vague ("the problem I care about" alone is too
     generic). Respect all of the user's honesty guardrails.
   - **Always offer a free-response "add your own" option** so the user can supply the mirror
     themselves; then shape their input to their voice and to the one-beat format.
4. **Recommend one** (recommend, don't survey) and ask for a single decision.
5. **After they pick, finalize the message in their voice**, email-first, honesty guardrails
   applied, and **avoid AI writing tells** (below).

## AI writing tells to avoid (sound human and like the user)

No em dashes. Avoid: "exact"/"exactly," "actually," "honestly," "delve," "tapestry," "underscore,"
"testament to," "in today's fast-paced world," "it's not just X, it's Y," robotic repeated "I was
impressed," empty superlatives ("incredible," "amazing" with nothing behind them), perfectly
balanced tricolons, over-hedging, and generic enthusiasm. Prefer one concrete, specific detail over
three vague ones. Read it out loud; if it doesn't sound like something the user would say to a
person, rewrite it. **Don't parrot the boss's exact phrases**, reference their accomplishment or
idea in the user's OWN words, with at most one short nod to their language. Over-quoting reads as
flattery, not kinship.

## Output

The 3 intersection points, 3 compliment approaches (with a recommendation), then, on the user's
pick, the finalized message.
