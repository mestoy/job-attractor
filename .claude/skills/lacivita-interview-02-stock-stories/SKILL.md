# Stage 2 — the stock stories (CA²R)

> Index and ground rules: `lacivita-interview-pipeline.md`. Andy's original wording is not
> reproduced here; build the candidate's own in their voice.

## What this stage is for

This is the heart of the method. Instead of guessing questions, the candidate builds a small
"speed rack" of true stories told in a fixed shape, then answers most behavioral questions by
selecting and aiming one. Six stories cover the large majority of what any interviewer asks.

## The formula: CA²R, and the doubled A is the point

Context, Approach, Approach, Result. It is CA²R, not CAAR and not STAR:

- **Context:** the situation and the stakes, two or three sentences, enough that a stranger cares.
- **Approach 1, the names:** the steps you took, NAMED in one pass, like a table of contents.
  "There were three parts: X, Y, Z." This is the move most people skip, and it is what makes a
  long answer easy to follow.
- **Approach 2, the detail:** now walk the named steps with the substance, one at a time. The
  listener already has the map, so depth reads as clarity instead of rambling.
- **Result:** what actually happened, with a real, defensible figure or outcome, plus what it
  meant for the customer or the business. End with a one sentence bridge aiming the story at the
  interviewer's world.

## The six stock stories

One story per slot, each from the candidate's verified record:

1. **Achievement:** the accomplishment they are proudest of, with the hardest evidence.
2. **Leadership:** a time they moved people without or beyond formal authority.
3. **Value:** a time their judgment created outsized value (a product call, a decision, a save).
4. **The mistake:** a real failure, what it cost, and what they changed because of it. The
   honesty is the content; a "weakness that is secretly a strength" answer is a tell.
5. **Service:** a time they served a customer or a colleague past the job description.
6. **Persistence:** a long slog that paid off, told so the grind is visible.

## The five selection rules

When choosing WHICH memory becomes the story for a slot:

1. Recent beats old, all else equal.
2. A story with a verifiable figure beats a story without one.
3. A story where the candidate is the actor beats one where they are the narrator.
4. Relevant to THIS employer's world beats impressive in general.
5. One story can serve two slots, but never tell it twice in one interview; the rack needs depth.

## Seniority note

The more senior the seat, the more the stories must carry judgment and outcomes rather than
effort, and the more the interviewer wants the future: senior conversations run mostly on "what
would you do here," with the stories as proof you have done it somewhere. For a senior candidate,
build every Result bridge to point forward at the employer's situation.

## Building the candidate's rack

1. **Ground in their documented record first.** Before offering any options, pull the candidate's
   locked story arcs, prior written tellings, and logged interview answers if the workspace has
   them. Their best telling usually already exists; find it before drafting anything new.
2. Co build one story at a time, one beat at a time, the candidate choosing or supplying the
   words. Never present a finished story for approval.
3. Verify every figure against the candidate's verified record before it enters a Result. An
   unsourced number does not ship.
4. Rehearse aloud. A story is ready when the candidate can tell it in under two minutes and can
   also give a thirty second version on request.

Output: six stories written down in the candidate's words, each tagged with the slots it can
serve and the bridge it ends on.
