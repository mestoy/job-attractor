# Stage 3 — the employer's questions

> Index and ground rules: `lacivita-interview-pipeline.md`. Andy's original wording is not
> reproduced here; build the candidate's own in their voice.

## What this stage is for

You cannot predict the question list, and trying produces brittle, recited answers. What you can
predict is the TYPE of thing interviewers ask, and every type maps to preparation you already did
in stages 1 and 2. This stage builds that map for a specific interview.

## The question types, and where each one routes

| Type | What they are checking | Route to |
|---|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | coherence, self awareness | stage 1 opener |
| Behavioral ("tell me about a time...") | evidence you have done it | a stage 2 story, aimed |
| Weakness, failure, conflict | honesty under pressure | story 4, the mistake, told straight |
| Hypothetical ("what would you do if...") | judgment | answer the future directly, then anchor with a story as proof |
| Why us, why this role | whether you chose them or sprayed | your stage 4 research, said back as reasons |
| Why did you leave / gaps | risk screening | a short, true, blame free account, rehearsed once, never improvised |
| Technical / craft depth | whether the résumé is real | honest depth, and a clean "here is where my edge ends" |

Two mechanics that raise every answer:

- **The value vocabulary.** Interviewers score answers against a small set of things employers
  actually buy: outcomes, judgment, speed, ownership, collaboration, growth. When choosing which
  story beat to emphasize, pick the one that speaks that vocabulary for THIS seat.
- **The time machine.** For any question about a past decision, briefly set the scene as it looked
  THEN, decide in that world, and only then connect to now. It keeps hindsight out of the answer
  and shows the judgment as it actually operated.

## Seniority note

Senior and founder conversations invert the ratio: most of the interview is the future (their
problems, your read, what you would do), and the past is the evidence base you dip into. For a
senior candidate, prepare to spend three quarters of the airtime forward looking, and keep the
stories to tight, aimed proof rather than centerpieces. Do not save a leadership story for a
behavioral prompt that may never come; if the leadership question arrives in future tense, answer
it in future tense and anchor with the story.

## Building the map for a specific interview

1. Pull the seat's JD and the interviewer's public work. List the five question types most likely
   for THIS pairing (a recruiter screen, a founder, a peer panel each skew differently).
2. For each likely type, note which stock story (or which stage 4 research point) it routes to,
   and the one beat to emphasize for this employer.
3. Rehearse the two answers that are unique to this interview: the "why us" and the "why did you
   leave." Everything else is the rack doing its job.
4. Keep the map to one page; it feeds the stage 5 day of card.

Output: a one page routing map, question type to story to emphasized beat, for this interview.
