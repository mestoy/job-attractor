# LaCivita networking — the relationship-distance ladder

> **This file is an INDEX, not a transcript.** Andy's original template wording lives in his
> purchased course material (*Networking Templates by Andrew LaCivita*, ©milewalk) and is not
> reproduced here. This captures the ladder, each template's situation and shape, and the
> mechanics that make them work, so you can build your own wording in your own voice for each rung.

## The core insight

The ten templates are organized by **relationship distance**, not by what you want. That ordering
*is* the method: the ask shrinks as the distance grows, and grows as the distance shrinks. A
stranger gets asked to connect. Someone who already works at the company gets asked for an
introduction. Do not skip the ladder and open with the big ask on a cold contact.

Most outreach programs run ONE rung (cold, no known opening) and never touch the warm half at all,
which is where a large share of a networking plan's effort is supposed to live. Running only the
cold rung and skipping the rest leaves half the method unused.

| # | Rung | Situation | The ask |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cold, not the boss | Found them researching; no connection at all | "at a minimum, I'd love to connect online and share networks" |
| 2 | Cold, not the boss (alt) | Same, shorter | connect on LinkedIn, then maybe coffee |
| 3 | **Cold, IS the boss** | An open position exists | "an opportunity to work directly for you as [title]" |
| 4 | **Cold, IS the boss** | No known opening | "whether there is an opportunity to work directly for you" |
| 5 | Warm 1st-degree | They know someone at the target | "any chance you could make a quick introduction?" |
| 6 | Warm 1st-degree | They **work** at the target | "make an introduction or route me to the right person" |
| 7 | Warm 1st-degree | Well-connected, unsure who they know | "do you have relationships at [Company 1, 2, or 3]?" |
| 8 | Referred 2nd-degree | Given a name at the target | "I got your name from [the person who referred you]" |
| 9 | Referred 2nd-degree | Given a name in general | "he/she immediately thought of you" |
| 10 | Event | Met briefly | reconnect + a "leave behind" |

If this kit's outreach scripts use a `--rung` flag, rungs 3-4 map to `cold-boss`, 1-2 map to
`cold-stranger`, 5-7 map to `warm`, 8-9 map to `referred`, and 10 maps to `event`.

### 🪜 A contact can be on more than one rung

**Rung classifies the ASK, never the person.** The same contact can hold several rungs open at
once. A 1st-degree contact at a company you would not take an offer from is closed at rungs 3-4
there, yet open at rungs 5-7 as a connector. A brand-new connection is 1st-degree (5-7 in reach)
yet held at 1-2 until the relationship has had time to develop. A connection-request target today
is a warm-rung target tomorrow, once they accept. So never store or look up "their rung": when you
pick a target, enumerate which rungs are OPEN for that person right now (how close you actually
are, how you were introduced, whether their employer is off-limits for you), pick the ask that
matches, and let the message depth and shape follow the rung of THAT ask.

### 🚫 When the employer is off-limits for you

Rungs 3-4 ask for *"an opportunity to work directly for you."* If you would decline an offer from
that company, that ask is dead. **The person moves sideways on the ladder, they do not drop off
it.**

| Their distance | Rung they move to | The ask becomes |
|---|---|---|
| 1st-degree | **5-7 (connector)** | *"do you have relationships at [Company 1, 2, or 3]?"* — rung 7 needs three named LIVE targets, so the off-limits company never appears in the ask |
| Not connected | **1-2, and no further** | connect and share networks, nothing more |
| Recent connection | **held at 1-2** | ask about THEIR work; never an intro ask, however warm the thread reads |

⛔ Never send rungs 3-4 to a contact at a company you would not take a job from, whatever the title
or how well the fit reads.

## The mechanics that make the warm rungs work

The difference between a warm ask that lands and one that dies is almost always about **removing
friction from the other person**.

- **Offer to write the intro for them.** *"I'd be happy to type up a few words for you to send."*
  The biggest friction in an intro request is composing it. Remove it for them.
- **Reframe the ask as giving them something.** If the target company pays an employee referral
  bonus, raise it explicitly. *"Nothing would thrill me more than for you to get something out of
  it!"* Turns a favor into a gift.
- **Name three companies, not a list.** *"Rather than toss you a long list, I figured I'd take a
  shot with a couple of my favorites."* A bounded ask is an easy yes.
- **Lower the bar on who counts.** *"It doesn't have to be the perfect alignment to your target
  unit. You just need an 'in.'"* Target the person who would actually be the likely boss, derived
  from how the company is built, not read off a title ladder.
- **Warm the opener to the real relationship.** *"I know it's been a while so I hope you're well!"*
  with an explicit note to adjust by how close you actually are. No pretending you talk more than
  you do.
- **Cold asks stay tiny.** The stranger template asks only to connect online; a live conversation
  is offered, never demanded. The ask escalates with the relationship, not with your need.

## Treat it as marketing testing

> *"Marketing is testing… keep a diligent tally, watch your modifications, play around with the
> tone."* · *"Which messages seem to be getting the best responses?"*

Most people run one register, keep no tally, and cannot say what works. Track outreach by register
(cold-boss vs. warm-1st-degree vs. referred) so you can see, over time, which one actually gets
replies.

## Andy's 12 target-list steps

1. Think · 2. Google · 3. Best-of lists · 4. Fastest-growing lists · 5. Schools, alumni services ·
6. Networking group lists · 7. LinkedIn browse · 8. LinkedIn contacts export · 9. Company review (of
your contacts) · 10. Person review (of your contacts) · 11. Aggregators (Indeed, CareerBuilder) ·
12. Glassdoor

Step 5 only applies if you have an alumni network to draw on; skip it if you do not.

## The invitation note — rung 1-2

A LinkedIn connection request carrying a note. The only thing this format can deliver is
**ACCEPTANCE**: no reply box, no signature, no room for the full set of ingredients in 300
characters. So the ask is template 1-2 and nothing more, and the channel is scored on acceptance
rather than replies.

**What tends to work:** a personalized opener, something that could only be sent to that specific
person, beats a mail-merged opener by a wide margin. A fact about your own use of their product, or
the specific problem their company solves in your own words, both work. Do **not** open on a claim
about yourself ("I'd be a great fit"); open on something about THEM.

### A working template shape

```
Hey, {First}!

I've already used {Product}, so this one is easy to root for.

I spent my early career in {the world their product serves} before this, which means the problem
you're solving is one I used to live in. Would love to connect!
```

**Fallback when you have not used their product:** open on the specific problem the company solves,
in your own framing. Never open on a claim about yourself; that pattern gets ignored.

### Rules that hold regardless of format

- Stay under 300 characters. LinkedIn truncates silently, mid-sentence, while still counting the
  send, so the margin can be a single clause.
- Never claim a capability or metric you cannot defend if asked about it directly.

⛔ **This does not relax anything about bosses.** Andy, verbatim: *"DO NOT send a LinkedIn
connection request to any bosses before you've made contact with them via these other techniques.
And, DO NOT send them a connection request unless you have their permission."* An acceptance is an
entry, not an outcome. The follow-through is a warm-rung message later, once the relationship
exists.

## The peer / common-interest note — rung 1-2, full body

A rung 1-2 note to someone you are **already connected to**: celebrate their public work, one
light give, one question. Unlike the invitation note above, this is not a connection request — it
is a normal-length message to an existing 1st-degree connection, with a real signature and no
character cap. It owes a genuine reason for reaching out and staying human; it does **not** owe
the cold-boss ask (who you are / why you chose them / what you want / a one-of-a-kind anchor).
That block is built for a work-for-you pitch, and a rung 1-2 note asks nothing of the kind.

If your kit's outreach linter has a `peer` message type, run it explicitly:
`python3 scripts/check_outreach.py <note.txt> --type peer`. Every other check (banned words, em
dash, retired figures, signature block) still applies — only the ingredient/one-of-a-kind check is
exempt for this type. A note run with no `--type` flag lints under the default first-contact
profile and will false-fail on the missing pitch ingredients.

⛔ **Not the same shape as the invitation note.** Invitation is a connection request to a stranger
(character-capped, no signature, ask = acceptance). Peer is a full-length note or email to an
**existing** connection.

## How to use this

1. Classify the target's rung first: is this person a stranger, a warm 1st-degree connection, a
   referral, or someone the boss you want to reach directly?
2. Pick the matching rung from the table above, then build the message in your own voice, one beat
   at a time, following your outreach construction skill.
3. Log which register was used, so a tally of what works actually means something.
